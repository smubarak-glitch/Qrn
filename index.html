    How the grid is built

    The text is split into words exactly as the Quranic Arabic Corpus splits it, which gives 77,429 words in 6,236 verses. The grid is the smallest near-square that holds them: 279 columns by 278 rows, with the last 133 squares left empty. Verses use 79 × 79 (6,236 of 6,241 squares) and the 604 pages of the Madani mushaf use 25 × 25.

    A dot is any word built on the root ش ه ر that the corpus tags as the noun shahr. In the Quran that root is only ever used for month, and it comes in four shapes: shahr (month), the dual shahrayn (two months), and the plurals ashhur and shuhūr (months).

    The revelation order sorts whole surahs by the traditional sequence Tanzil lists. Verses inside each surah keep their usual order.

    Checks

    • A plain spelling search of the Tanzil text for ش ه ر, plus ش ه و ر for shuhūr, finds the same 21 words in the same 17 verses as the root search.
    • Ramadan, the month of fasting, is named once, in 2:185, which also contains shahr twice.
    • Saheeh International, the translation shown here, adds the word month in brackets in two more verses, 2:196 and 9:37. Other translations add it in other places. The Arabic word is not there, so those verses get no dot.
